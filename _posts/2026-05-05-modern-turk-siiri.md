While the world was exploring Modernism in painting, a group of Turkish poets in the 1950s—including Cemal Süreya, Edip Cansever, and Ece Ayhan—were launching their own silent revolution. They called it *İkinci Yeni* (The Second New).

### 🖋️ Breaking the Chains of Meaning
The *İkinci Yeni* poets moved away from the simplicity of everyday language. They embraced surrealism and the subconscious, creating images that were often difficult to decipher but impossible to forget. As Edip Cansever once said, "A man lives where he is most lost."

### ✨ A Vintage Aesthetic of Words
Their poetry is like an abstract painting; it doesn't tell you a story—it makes you feel a state of being. They used metaphors like complex labyrinths, exploring loneliness, urban life, and the deep intricacies of the human heart. For the modern reader, their verses remain a timeless sanctuary of intellectual and emotional depth.
