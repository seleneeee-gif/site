    <h2 style="color: #8e44ad;">From Cave Walls to Canvas</h2>
    <p>Art history is not just a collection of dates and names; it is the visual diary of humanity. For thousands of years, humans have used art to explain the unexplainable, to capture beauty, and to protest injustice. From the flickering shadows of Lascaux Cave to the divine ceilings of the Sistine Chapel, the evolution of art reflects the evolution of our collective consciousness.</p>

    <h3 style="color: #d35400;">The Renaissance: The Birth of Realism</h3>
    <p>During the 15th century, the Renaissance marked a profound shift toward humanism. Artists like Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo rediscovered the mathematical beauty of the human body and the laws of perspective. This era was defined by the belief that man was the measure of all things, leading to an obsession with anatomical precision and the delicate balance of light and shadow, known as <em>chiaroscuro</em>.</p>

    <h3 style="color: #d35400;">Modernism and the Deconstruction of Reality</h3>
    <p>As we entered the 20th century, the world became more complex. Traditional realism was no longer enough to express the chaos of modern life. Movements like Cubism, led by Pablo Picasso, began to deconstruct objects into geometric shapes, while Abstract Expressionism sought to bypass the conscious mind entirely. Today, art continues to evolve, using digital tools and AI to ask the same eternal question: <strong>What does it mean to be human?</strong></p>
